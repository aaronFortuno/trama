## L246 · Reserves del projector

Use case: illustration-story. Asset: one landscape 3:2 illustration for a first-year secondary-school reading in the Trama catalogue, in the recurring universe of a neighbourhood library of things. Create an original editorial gouache and coloured-pencil painting on lightly mottled warm cream paper: fine paper grain, careful realistic object geometry, matte textures, muted teal-green walls, honey oak wood, ochre and terracotta accents, warm afternoon light, calm lived-in atmosphere. Scene: a shared object-lending library counter. A compact light-grey video projector is unplugged and switched off, its lens and front face pointing plausibly toward the viewer, its neatly coiled black power cable lying next to its open padded carrying case. A closed teal booking ledger and three separate blank cream reservation cards lie on the wooden counter, along with a simple round wall clock in the background. Background shelves hold a sewing machine, folded blankets, a green camping lantern and baskets; an arched green-framed window and a trailing plant connect this room with the library's established visual identity. Natural perspective and clear, uncluttered composition, objects resting fully on surfaces. This is a literary illustration about making fair reservations, not a technical diagram. No people or hands, no beam of light from the switched-off projector, no readable lettering, numbers, logos or watermarks. Preserve quiet tactile detail and readable silhouettes when printed small.

## L247 · Retorn de la màquina

Use case: illustration-story. Create one landscape 3:2 original editorial gouache and coloured-pencil illustration on warm, finely grained cream paper for a school reading in the shared universe of a neighbourhood library of things. Match a tactile storybook style with realistic, carefully constructed objects, muted teal-green walls, honey oak wood, ochre and terracotta accents, warm natural light and trailing houseplants. Subject: an unpowered cream-coloured domestic sewing machine resting on a broad wooden lending-library counter, alongside a completely disconnected black foot pedal with its cable neatly coiled and connector visible; a soft open teal carrying bag that is large enough to hold the machine sits beside it. A small cream card and pencil lie flat on the counter, suggesting a careful return inspection. Keep machine mechanism simple and plausible: stable base, one sewing arm, needle above the metal sewing bed, no tangled thread. In the softly detailed background are sturdy wooden shelves with woven baskets and folded fabric, part of a green-framed arched window, and a potted vine. Quiet uncluttered composition, all objects supported by the counter, no people, no hands, no powered equipment, no readable text, numbers, logos or watermarks. This is an illustration about describing an object fairly when returning it, not an operating diagram.

## L248 · Els dos tamborets

Use case: illustration-story. Asset: one landscape 3:2 original editorial gouache and coloured-pencil painting for a reading set in a neighbourhood library of things. Keep a realistic, tactile storybook look on finely grained warm paper, muted teal-green walls, honey oak shelves, ochre and terracotta colours, natural late-afternoon light, potted vines and a green-framed arched window. Scene: two different wooden stools standing fully on the terracotta floor in front of the lending counter, seen from a clear three-quarter angle. The stool on the left is low and worn, with a plain square seat, four straight legs and sensible crossbars. The stool on the right is a substantially taller pale-wood stool with a round seat, four coherent legs and low crossbars; it is about one and three-quarter times the height of the low stool. Both have physically plausible construction and perspective, with legs attached to their own seats and resting on the same floor. On the counter behind them are two small blank inventory cards, a pencil and a closed green ledger. Shelves show a basket, folded fabric and a camping lantern, all secondary to the stools. The image supports a discussion about which donated objects a shared library can actually use; it is not a repair diagram. No people, hands, readable text, measurements, numbers, logos or watermarks. Avoid floating legs, impossible joins and duplicated structural parts.
